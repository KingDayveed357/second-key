Second Key: the second key is held by someone else (ADR-0020)

A payer's app can no longer approve a payment that needs their trusted
contact, read the contact's messages, or obtain the contact's credential.
This is enforced in the engine, inside the row-locked transaction that
applies the decision, not by the UI or the proxy.

Before this change the reviewer token was returned to the payer
(hold-link), the payer's own key could call /approve, the "rule set in
advance" was a per-payment intent field, and first_payment was asserted by
the caller.

What changes:
- Standing protection profile per payer (threshold, guardian). Tightening
  applies at once; loosening or replacing the guardian waits 48 h (server
  time) and the current guardian is told. Every screened transfer snapshots
  the profile version and threshold it was checked under.
- Enrolled guardians: invite, accept through their own channel, assign.
  Emergency revocation is immediate and requires a reason; re-granting
  authority after that is still delayed.
- Who may approve is decided when the hold is created, by deterministic
  reason classes (POLICY_REVIEW, EVIDENCE_CONCERN, ASSESSMENT_UNAVAILABLE,
  LISTING_OR_FACTS). Payer or admin approval of a guardian hold -> 403,
  audited. Payer and admin can always cancel.
- Credentials are minted at the channel's last hop, stored only as SHA-256,
  bound to one hold and one guardian. They expire in 30 min, are replaced
  by a reminder, replacement or revocation, and lock after 10 wrong
  attempts. They never travel in a URL query: POST body or link fragment
  only.
- Transactional notification outbox (notifications table + pg-boss job in
  the same transaction), with bounded retries. A delivery failure never
  grants the payer approval.
- Payee history is written only when a transfer is CAPTURED (or by an
  audited import). The payee key includes the payout email, so a known id
  with a new email is a new payee. The intent can only make the rule
  stricter.
- Lock order profile -> purchase -> hold -> audit. This fixes a deadlock
  (40P01) between revocation and approval that a concurrency test caught.
- Demo: a payer per visitor (httpOnly cookie), and Sarah's phone as its own
  window holding its own inbox secret. Labelled as a demo channel that
  proves the API boundary, not channel security.
- Migration 0006: new tables; existing holds become 'legacy' (old
  behaviour, auto-void within 72 h); captured transfers backfill payee
  history.

Tests: 22 new guardian tests (self-approval, inbox isolation, credential
lifecycle, replacement, removal and revocation with holds pending,
concurrent races, notification failure, AI unavailable, tenant isolation,
profile semantics, payee spoofing). Existing tests updated where the
contract changed. typecheck, deps:check, 509 vitest and 16 node tests pass.
The browser rehearsal ran against fake PayPal locally; the change has not
been re-run against the PayPal sandbox on Render.
